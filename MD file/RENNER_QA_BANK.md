# Lojas Renner QC Master Q&A Bank

This document contains 300 detailed questions and answers covering real-field scenarios, common inspector mistakes, Youcom Menswear exceptions, Inspectorio CAPA processes, and mixed guidelines reviews.

## Topic: FIELD-SCENARIOS (80 Questions)

### Q: Inspector arrives at factory and finds only 60% of production is ready. AQL quantity requires 200 pcs. What should the inspector do?
**A:** Inspector must NOT start inspection. Minimum 80% production must be ready. Factory should be notified, inspection postponed, and office informed. Report must note the situation.

### Q: During final inspection, inspector finds the sealed sample is missing at the factory. How should the inspector proceed?
**A:** Inspector must contact the QC office immediately. Without a sealed sample, inspection cannot be fully validated. Inspector should document missing sealed sample in the report and put result on HOLD pending office decision.

### Q: Inspector finds factory has mixed 2 different colorways in the same packing box. What action is taken?
**A:** This is a LOGISTICS defect (wrong assortment). The entire shipment must be rechecked. This is likely a REJECT unless all boxes can be corrected before shipment.

### Q: During POM check, 3 out of 5 pieces show chest measurement 2cm over tolerance. What is the decision?
**A:** Fail on POM. The inspector records all deviations, photographs the measurement process, and issues a HOLD or REJECT depending on AQL limits. POM failures cannot be accepted without buyer approval.

### Q: Inspector arrives and factory requests to inspect only 'good' pre-selected pieces. What does the inspector do?
**A:** REFUSE. Samples must be selected RANDOMLY by the inspector from the production, NOT pre-selected by the factory. Allowing pre-selection invalidates the inspection. This must be reported.

### Q: The barcode scanner shows 'invalid barcode' on 15% of units. What is the inspector's action?
**A:** RFID/barcode failure on 15% is a critical logistics defect. Record quantity, take photos. Result should be HOLD or REJECT. Factory must reprogram/replace all affected units before shipping.

### Q: Inspector finds a critical VX11 defect (free ends on hood/neck area) in a children's garment. Only 1 piece affected. What is the decision?
**A:** IMMEDIATE REJECT. VX safety defects in children's garments are ZERO TOLERANCE. Even 1 piece with VX11 means the entire style must be REJECTED. No acceptance possible without redesign confirmation.

### Q: During inspection, factory manager says the buyer already approved a deviation by phone. How should the inspector handle this?
**A:** Inspector cannot accept verbal approvals. Only written/email approval from the QC office or buyer representative is valid. Inspection should proceed per standard manual. Document the factory's claim in the report.

### Q: Inspector finds hangers are the correct style but missing the Renner price tag printed on the hanger. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. Renner-specific hanger markings are part of the packaging requirement. Missing hanger tags = packaging defect. Inspector must record and consult with office if this is a HOLD or REJECT.

### Q: An inspector is asked to inspect 2 different styles on the same day at the same factory. Production is ready for both. What is the correct approach?
**A:** Inspector can inspect both styles in one visit. However, each style requires its own AQL sampling, separate report, and independent result. They cannot be combined into one report.

### Q: Factory shows inspector a Corrective Action Report from a previous inspection. They say old issues are fixed. Should inspector skip checking those points?
**A:** NO. Inspector must independently verify all points in the current inspection. Previous CAPA reports are reference only. Every inspection starts fresh with the full checklist.

### Q: Inspector finds 20% of polybags have holes/tears. Is this an operational or visual defect?
**A:** Damaged polybag is an OPERATIONAL defect (packaging integrity). It must be recorded. If AQL limits are exceeded, result is HOLD. Factory must replace all damaged polybags.

### Q: During inspection of jeans, inspector notices heavy chlorine smell. Garments appear normal visually. What should inspector do?
**A:** Inspector should note the smell in the report. Strong chemical smell may indicate improper washing/finishing. Inspector should escalate to QC office. This can be a lab test trigger for chemical compliance.

### Q: Inspector's tablet/device fails during inspection. No digital report can be submitted. What is the correct action?
**A:** Inspector must complete a manual/paper report documenting all findings. Photos must be saved locally. Report must be submitted to office immediately after returning or using alternative device. Inspection result cannot be delayed.

### Q: Factory presents 3 different fabric lots for same style. Only 1 lot was used in bulk. Which lot should inspector sample from?
**A:** Inspector must sample ONLY from the bulk production lot. If factory cannot confirm which lot is which, inspector must flag this as a traceability issue and consult the QC office.

### Q: During accessories inspection, inspector finds belt length measures correctly but metal buckle has sharp edges. What is the defect classification?
**A:** Sharp metal edges on buckles = CRITICAL safety defect (especially if children's product). For adult products, it is a MAJOR defect. Photo must be taken and result should be HOLD/REJECT depending on product category.

### Q: Inspector finds some cartons have wrong destination port printed on master carton label. Garments inside are correct. Decision?
**A:** Wrong master carton label = LOGISTICS defect. This cannot be shipped as-is. Result: HOLD. Factory must reprint and relabel all affected cartons. Inspector must recheck labels before passing.

### Q: A factory has 3,000 pcs ready. AQL Level II requires 125 pcs sample. Inspector only checks 80 pcs due to time pressure. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. AQL sample size is MANDATORY. Reducing sample size invalidates the statistical basis of the inspection. Inspector must check full 125 pcs or report why inspection was incomplete. Time pressure is NOT a valid reason.

### Q: Inspector finds size label sewn inside garment shows 'M' but the hanger size strip shows 'L'. What is this defect?
**A:** Size label mismatch between inside label and hanger = MAJOR defect. This causes consumer confusion and returns. Result: HOLD or REJECT. All units must be checked and corrected.

### Q: During footwear inspection, inspector notices the left and right shoes of same pair have slightly different shade of leather. What action?
**A:** Shade variation between pair components = MAJOR visual defect. Inspector must check multiple pairs. If confirmed across batch, result is HOLD/REJECT. Shade consistency is a critical footwear quality standard.

### Q: Factory asks inspector to sign a 'pre-inspection agreement' form before starting. What should the inspector do?
**A:** Inspector must NOT sign any factory documents. The only form inspector signs is the official Renner/Movimoda inspection report. Signing factory forms may create legal or contractual obligations. Refuse and report to office.

### Q: Inspector finds 5% of pieces have RFID tag in wrong position (shifted 3cm from spec). Is this a critical defect?
**A:** Yes. RFID position is critical for scanner read accuracy at Renner stores. Wrong position = functional failure. This is a MAJOR logistics defect. If AQL limit exceeded, result is HOLD/REJECT.

### Q: During inspection, inspector finds price sticker partially covers the composition label. Composition is still readable. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. Composition/care labels must be fully visible and unobstructed per Renner packaging requirements. Partially covered labels = MINOR to MAJOR defect. Factory must reposition price stickers.

### Q: A style has 4 colorways. AQL passed for 3 colorways. 1 colorway has too many defects. What is the final result?
**A:** Each colorway is treated independently. The 3 passing colorways = PASS. The 1 failing colorway = HOLD/REJECT. Factory can ship passing colorways only if office approves. Failing colorway must be addressed separately.

### Q: Inspector finds care label language is only in Portuguese. No Spanish or English. Shipment is for Brazilian market. Is this acceptable?
**A:** For Brazilian domestic market, Portuguese-only care labels are acceptable. Renner is a Brazilian retailer. However, inspector should confirm this against the approved sample/spec sheet to ensure compliance.

### Q: Factory has 500 pcs ready. Inspector randomly selects sample. Factory insists inspector check their 'best' 50 pcs first. What to do?
**A:** Reject factory's request. Random sampling is MANDATORY. Factory cannot guide sample selection. Inspector must maintain independence. Document factory interference attempt in the report.

### Q: Inspector finds button pull test fails — button detaches with less than 90N force. How many buttons must be tested minimum?
**A:** Minimum 3 buttons from 3 different garments per the accessories test requirement. If any button fails 90N pull test, this is a MAJOR defect (critical for children's garments). Result: HOLD/REJECT.

### Q: During inspection, factory claims this is a 'rush order' and asks inspector to skip some checklist points. Response?
**A:** Rush order status does NOT reduce inspection requirements. Full checklist must be completed. If time is limited, inspector should focus on critical points first but must document incomplete areas and inform office.

### Q: Inspector finds stitch density in seams is 10 SPI instead of required 12 SPI. This is found in 40% of checked pieces. Decision?
**A:** Stitch density deviation in 40% of pieces = MAJOR operational defect. This affects seam strength and durability. Result: HOLD. Factory must re-examine production parameters and confirm correction.

### Q: Inspector is checking sunglasses. Finds UV protection sticker missing on 8% of pieces. Is this a critical, major, or minor defect?
**A:** Missing UV protection sticker = CRITICAL defect for sunglasses. UV protection labeling is a safety/regulatory requirement. Even 1 piece missing this label is serious. AQL 0 applies. Result: REJECT/HOLD immediately.

### Q: Inspector finds the bulk fabric has a different shade compared to approved sealed sample under natural light. Under artificial light, they match. Decision?
**A:** Inspector must check under BOTH lighting conditions. If shade varies under natural light, this is a MAJOR visual defect. Inspector must photograph both conditions. Result: HOLD. Buyer must approve or reject the deviation.

### Q: Factory presents a revised spec sheet printed by their own team, claiming buyer approved changes. Inspector has only the original spec. What to do?
**A:** Inspector uses ONLY the original approved spec sheet from QC office. Factory-produced spec revisions are NOT valid without official written confirmation from the QC office or buyer. Proceed with original spec.

### Q: Inspector finds 3 cartons out of 50 are wet/damp due to storage. Contents appear dry. Should inspector accept?
**A:** NO. Wet/damp cartons are a LOGISTICS defect — risk of mold, label damage, barcode unreadability. Record all affected cartons. Result: HOLD until cartons are replaced and contents verified dry.

### Q: Inspector finds 100% of polybags have correct garment inside but the polybag print (buyer logo) is on the wrong side (facing inward). Decision?
**A:** Wrong polybag orientation = MAJOR packaging defect. The buyer logo/design must face outward. This requires correction of all units. Result: HOLD. Factory must repack all garments.

### Q: Factory has complete packing but 5% of master cartons have no strap/tape seal. Cartons can be opened by hand. Action?
**A:** Unsealed cartons = LOGISTICS defect. They risk opening during transport and potential content loss/damage. Result: HOLD on affected cartons. Factory must tape/seal all cartons per spec before shipping.

### Q: Inspector is conducting an inline inspection when factory shows batch of 200 pcs mid-production. 12% have broken stitches. What happens?
**A:** 12% broken stitches in an inline is a MAJOR inline defect. Production must STOP. Factory should investigate root cause (needle, thread tension, machine issue). Inline results go to office for decision. May require rework before inline passes.

### Q: During RFID validation, 2% of tags are read but with wrong data (correct style, wrong size). How critical is this?
**A:** Wrong RFID data = CRITICAL logistics defect. Even 2% wrong data causes inventory management failures at Renner stores. Result: HOLD/REJECT. All units must be verified and tags must be reprogrammed.

### Q: Inspector checks handbag handles. One side attachment has 4 stitches, other side has 6 stitches. Is this defective?
**A:** Unequal stitching on bag handles = MAJOR visual/operational defect (appearance inconsistency + strength concern). Inspector must check across multiple pieces. If consistent issue, result: HOLD.

### Q: A children's garment has a decorative cord at waist level. Cord measures 22cm hanging free. Manual maximum is 14cm. Decision?
**A:** VX7 defect — cord/belt longer than maximum allowed. This is a SAFETY DEFECT. Result: IMMEDIATE REJECT. Children's garments with long cords at waist = strangulation risk. Zero tolerance.

### Q: Inspector finds some pieces have composition '100% Cotton' but fabric feels like polyester blend. What is the correct action?
**A:** Suspected composition mismatch requires lab testing. Inspector must flag this as a HIGH CONCERN in the report. Result: HOLD pending lab test. Burn test can give preliminary indication but is not official. Escalate to QC office immediately.

### Q: Factory offers inspector a 'gift' or 'lunch' before inspection starts. What is the ethical response?
**A:** Inspector must REFUSE any gifts or hospitality that could compromise objectivity. This is an ethical violation. Accepting gifts from factories is prohibited under inspector code of conduct. Inspector should report the offer to their supervisor.

### Q: Inspector finds outer garment passes all checks but lining fabric is heavily wrinkled from folding. Is this a defect?
**A:** Heavy lining wrinkle = MINOR visual defect if it steams/presses out easily. If permanent wrinkle/crease = MAJOR. Inspector should steam test 1 piece. Document and photograph finding either way.

### Q: All 125 sampled pieces pass AQL. But inspector visually notices 1 entire production table has a systematic sewing error affecting ~200 pcs. What to do?
**A:** Inspector must flag the systematic error even though AQL sample passed. Random sample may not have captured these 200 pcs. Inspector should report the cluster defect to office. Office may request 100% check of affected table production.

### Q: Inspector finds the Inspectorio app is not loading. QC report cannot be submitted digitally. What is the protocol?
**A:** Complete inspection manually (paper/photos). Contact QC office to report technical issue. Submit manual report as soon as possible. Do NOT delay inspection results due to technical issues. QC office will advise on manual submission procedure.

### Q: Factory says 200 pcs were inspected by their own QC team and passed. They ask Renner inspector to only spot check 20 pcs. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. Renner inspection is independent of factory's internal QC. Factory self-inspection does not reduce or replace the AQL sample requirement. Full AQL sample must be drawn by the inspector.

### Q: Inspector finds 10% of pieces have a very faint print on chest logo — visible only in direct light. How to classify?
**A:** Faint/weak print = MAJOR visual defect if it does not match the sealed sample print quality. Inspector must compare directly with sealed sample. If significantly below standard = MAJOR. Document and photograph in different light angles.

### Q: During shoes inspection, inspector finds heel height measures 5.2cm. Spec says 5cm ±0.3cm. Is this within tolerance?
**A:** YES. 5.2cm is within ±0.3cm tolerance of 5cm (range: 4.7cm–5.3cm). No defect. Record the measurement as within spec. Inspector should still document the measured value in the report.

### Q: Inspector finds that the garment's country of origin label says 'Bangladesh' but the factory is in Vietnam. What action?
**A:** CRITICAL mislabeling defect. Country of origin must be accurate. This may have legal/customs implications. Result: IMMEDIATE HOLD/REJECT. Escalate to QC office urgently. Do not allow shipment until corrected.

### Q: An inline inspection at 20% production shows 8% defect rate. Factory wants to continue production. Should inspector allow this?
**A:** 8% defect rate at inline is typically above acceptable threshold. Inspector should recommend STOPPING production for root cause analysis. If factory continues without fixing, result is recorded as FAIL inline. Office must be notified.

### Q: Garment's wash care symbol shows tumble dry. But composition is 100% linen. Is this a concern?
**A:** YES. 100% linen is NOT recommended for tumble drying as it causes shrinkage and damage. This is a likely mislabeled care instruction = MAJOR defect. Inspector must flag and hold pending buyer/technical team confirmation.

### Q: Inspector receives a complaint that a passed style had massive returns at Renner stores. Inspector is asked to re-inspect. What is the approach?
**A:** Re-inspection is valid. Inspector should focus on the reported defect category, draw fresh AQL sample, and verify if the defect is still present in remaining stock. Document findings objectively without bias.

### Q: During accessories inspection, clasp on necklace breaks during normal handling. AQL sample = 5 breakages in 200 pieces. Result?
**A:** Breaking clasps = CRITICAL functional defect. Exceeding AQL for functional failures = REJECT. All units must be checked or replaced. Factory must resolve clasp quality before any shipment.

### Q: Inspector finds master carton GW (Gross Weight) marked as 8.5 kg. Actual weight measured = 9.3 kg. Difference = 0.8 kg. Is this acceptable?
**A:** Weight discrepancy >5% is typically a LOGISTICS defect. 0.8/8.5 = ~9.4% variance. Inspector must record actual weights. Flag as MAJOR logistics issue. Correct carton labels required before shipping.

### Q: During inspection of leather bags, inspector finds uneven leather grain pattern between bag panels from same style. Decision?
**A:** Uneven grain pattern between panels = MAJOR visual defect. Different grain direction/texture looks unfinished. Inspector must check all sampled pieces. If systematic issue, result: HOLD.

### Q: Inspector finds some embroidery patches are lifting at edges. They are bonded, not stitched. How serious is this?
**A:** Lifting bonded embroidery = MAJOR defect (durability and appearance). Bonded items may detach in washing. Inspector must check bonding strength. If patches detach easily by hand = CRITICAL. Result: HOLD/REJECT.

### Q: Factory cannot provide the original approved trim card. They show a photo on their phone of the trim card. Is this acceptable for comparison?
**A:** Phone photos of trim cards are NOT acceptable as reference. Official trim approval must be physical or printed from official approved source. Inspector should compare with sealed sample instead. Flag missing trim card in report.

### Q: Inspector finds 5 pieces from a 125 AQL sample have correct construction but show 'run marks' from fabric production. Classification?
**A:** Run marks = MAJOR fabric defect if visible from 50cm. Inspector must classify per defect table. If run marks appear in more than AQL 2.5 limit for majors, result is HOLD. Document location and severity for each piece.

### Q: Inspector finds the PLM system shows 'APPROVED' for the style but the spec sheet inspector has is dated 3 months earlier. Conflict. What to do?
**A:** Inspector must use the most recent approved spec sheet. Contact QC office to confirm the latest version. Do not proceed with outdated specs. All inspections must reference the current approved version from official sources.

### Q: Factory shows inspector a WhatsApp screenshot from a buyer saying 'the color is fine.' Should inspector accept this as buyer approval?
**A:** NO. WhatsApp screenshots are not official approvals. Only formal written approvals from the QC office or official buyer channels (email with proper authorization) are valid. Inspector cannot accept social media screenshots as spec approvals.

### Q: Inspector checks 200 units of footwear. 5 pairs have sole delaminating at heel. AQL Level II limit for majors is 5 in 200. Exactly on limit. What is the decision?
**A:** Exactly meeting AQL limit = MARGINAL PASS statistically. However, delaminating soles are a CRITICAL safety/durability issue. Inspector should consult QC office. Conservative approach = HOLD. Document exact count and severity photographically.

### Q: Inspector finds trim labels showing '100% Organic Cotton' but buyer has not certified organic content. Is this a concern?
**A:** YES. Unverified organic claims = CRITICAL regulatory compliance risk. False organic labeling has legal implications. Inspector must flag this. Result: HOLD. QC office and buyer must verify organic certification before any 'organic' claim is on labels.

### Q: Inspector finds zipper pull engraving shows 'Renner' misspelled as 'Reneer.' How to classify?
**A:** Buyer brand misspelling = CRITICAL branding/identity defect. This cannot be shipped under any circumstances. Result: IMMEDIATE REJECT. All zippers with misspelling must be replaced.

### Q: Factory presents 3,000 pieces. AQL 1.0 for criticals (0 acceptance). Inspector finds 0 critical, 4 major, 2 minor. AQL II for major is 5 in 125 sample. Result?
**A:** 0 criticals = PASS for critical. 4 majors in 125 = within AQL 2.5 limit (accept ≤7). 2 minors = well within limits. FINAL RESULT: PASS. All counts are within acceptance numbers.

### Q: During inspection, inspector notices that workers are assembling unsewn garments in the same room as finished goods. Contamination risk?
**A:** Cross-contamination between WIP and finished goods is a factory floor management issue. Inspector should note this in the report as an observation. If fiber/thread contamination is found on finished goods = defect. Report conditions to QC office.

### Q: Inspector finds all sizes present and correct but the size run ratio in cartons does not match the approved ratio (S:M:L = 2:3:1 vs approved 1:2:1). Decision?
**A:** Wrong size ratio in cartons = LOGISTICS defect. Even if individual sizes are correct, wrong ratio will cause stock imbalance at stores. Result: HOLD. Factory must repack to match approved size ratio.

### Q: Inspector is checking Renner-branded footwear. Box is correct but shoe insole has third-party brand name. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. Mixed brand identity = MAJOR defect. Renner product should not show competitor or unrelated brand names. Inspector must record and report. Result: HOLD pending buyer decision on corrective action.

### Q: During inspection, factory provides sealed sample that is torn/damaged and unclear on several areas. Can inspector use this sample?
**A:** Damaged sealed sample complicates comparison. Inspector should document the damaged sample condition with photos. Use the best available areas of the sample for comparison. Report issue to QC office. Request new/replacement sealed sample if possible.

### Q: Inspector finds 3% of pieces have color staining on inside lining from outer fabric. Staining removes with water. Classify?
**A:** Color bleed/staining from outer to lining = MAJOR defect (crocking/bleeding concern). Even if it removes with water, the risk during consumer use remains. Result: HOLD. Colorfastness lab test may be required.

### Q: Factory says they need inspection result in 2 hours because truck leaves. Inspector has not finished. What to do?
**A:** Truck schedules do NOT override inspection thoroughness. Inspector must complete full inspection or clearly document what was/was not checked. If incomplete, result cannot be PASS. Inspector must communicate timeline to QC office, not to factory.

### Q: Inspector finds inner polybag is correct but outer hanging bag for presentation is missing. Spec requires outer hanging bag. Decision?
**A:** Missing outer hanging bag = PACKAGING defect per spec. Result: HOLD. Factory must add correct outer hanging bags. Inspector should verify this against spec/approved sample before final release.

### Q: During inspection of 125 pieces, inspector finds 1 garment with a sewing needle fragment inside pocket. AQL defect count?
**A:** Embedded needle = CRITICAL SAFETY DEFECT. AQL count: 1 critical. For criticals, AQL acceptance number = 0. IMMEDIATE REJECT. Factory must conduct 100% metal detection of ALL production units before any shipment.

### Q: Inspector finds polybag size does not match the garment — garment is too tight inside the bag. The bag is correctly labeled. Is this acceptable?
**A:** Incorrect polybag size = MINOR-MAJOR packaging defect. Garment may wrinkle/crease from tight packaging. If garment arrives wrinkled to consumer = quality complaint. Inspector should flag and recommend correct polybag sizing.

### Q: Inspector visits factory and finds production not started yet. Factory promises to produce in next 2 days. What does inspector do?
**A:** Inspector must leave and report to office. Inspection cannot happen with zero production. Office will reschedule. Factory should be advised that PPM/production planning must be better coordinated. Inspector records visit attempt.

### Q: Inspector finds Renner price tag shows R$89.90 but production order says R$79.90. Which price is correct?
**A:** Inspector cannot determine correct price. Inspector must put result on HOLD and contact QC office immediately. Wrong price tag = CRITICAL retail/pricing error. Office must confirm with buyer which price is correct before any shipment.

### Q: Factory produces 2,000 pieces but can only show 800 pcs at inspection because rest are in transit between factory buildings. What to do?
**A:** Inspector must ONLY inspect pieces physically present. If 800 pcs present, AQL sample must be drawn from these 800 pcs using correct sample size. Pieces not physically available = not inspected. This must be clearly documented in report.

### Q: Inspector samples a style with hand embroidery. Some pieces have slight variation in embroidery density. Is variation acceptable for handmade items?
**A:** Minor variations in hand embroidery are generally accepted as natural variation of handcrafted items. However, if variation is beyond the range shown in sealed sample = MAJOR. Inspector compares with sealed sample range for judgment.

### Q: During a re-inspection after CAPA, factory has corrected the original defect but now has new defects at different points. How is re-inspection reported?
**A:** Re-inspection must cover all original defect points PLUS full checklist. New defects found are recorded normally. If new defects exceed AQL limits, re-inspection = FAIL. Factory cannot simply fix old issues and create new ones.

### Q: Inspector finds 100% of pieces pass AQL but 2 pieces have critical defects. These 2 were not in the AQL sample. Inspector found them by chance after AQL. What is the decision?
**A:** Any critical defect found — even outside AQL sample — cannot be ignored. Inspector must report the criticals. The 2 critical defects found may indicate systemic issue. Result: HOLD/REJECT. Factory must investigate source of criticals.

### Q: Inspector finds that all checked pieces have correct color but 1 carton has a completely different fabric texture — likely wrong fabric lot mixed in. Action?
**A:** Wrong fabric lot = CRITICAL traceability/quality defect. Inspector must isolate the affected carton(s). Check if more cartons are affected. Result: HOLD for affected cartons. Investigation required. May extend to checking all cartons.

### Q: Inspector checks 200 footwear pairs. 3 pairs have slightly asymmetrical toe box shape — one side slightly more rounded. Defect level?
**A:** Asymmetrical toe box = MAJOR visual/construction defect if visible beyond acceptable tolerance. Inspector should compare with sealed sample. If sealed sample also has slight asymmetry, it may be within design. Document with photos for office review.

## Topic: INSPECTOR-MISTAKES (60 Questions)

### Q: Inspector accepts factory's pre-selected 'best' sample and passes inspection. What rule was violated?
**A:** RULE VIOLATED: Random sampling requirement. Inspector must ALWAYS select samples randomly and independently from the production. Accepting pre-selected samples invalidates inspection integrity and violates AQL sampling standards.

### Q: Inspector only checks visual defects and skips POM measurements to save time. Is this acceptable?
**A:** NO. Skipping POMs is a MAJOR inspection error. POM checks are mandatory for Renner inspections. Inspector must check all four pillars: AQL quality, POMs, RFID/barcodes, and logistics/packaging. Incomplete inspection = invalid result.

### Q: Inspector marks a VX safety defect as 'minor' because the garment is for adults, not children. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. VX codes are specifically for children's garments. For adult garments, different defect codes apply. Applying VX codes to adult garments AND misclassifying severity are both inspection errors. Use correct defect code system per product category.

### Q: Inspector uses a stretch tape measure for garment POM measurements. Correct tool?
**A:** WRONG. Stretch tape measures introduce inaccuracy. Inspector must use a NON-STRETCH (fiberglass/cloth) flat tape measure for all garment POM checks. Using wrong measurement tools = unreliable POM results.

### Q: Inspector finds 8 major defects in 125 pieces. AQL 2.5 allows maximum 7 majors. Inspector passes the inspection because '8 is close to 7.' Correct?
**A:** WRONG. AQL acceptance numbers are FIRM limits, not approximations. 8 majors when limit is 7 = FAIL. Inspector cannot use personal judgment to override statistical AQL limits. The result must be HOLD/REJECT.

### Q: Inspector accepts a phone WhatsApp message from factory manager as proof that buyer approved a specification deviation. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Only formal written approvals from QC office/official buyer channels are valid. WhatsApp messages are not official approval documents. Inspector must follow original spec until formal written deviation approval is received.

### Q: Inspector forgets to check RFID functionality and only visually verifies tags are present. Is this sufficient?
**A:** NO. Visual presence of RFID tags is NOT sufficient. Inspector must scan/verify RFID tags are programmed with correct data and are readable. Non-functional RFID tags are a critical logistics failure even if physically present.

### Q: Inspector checks only the top layer of cartons and passes without checking cartons from middle and bottom. Mistake?
**A:** YES. This is a common but serious mistake. Defects may be concentrated in specific areas of production. Inspector must draw samples RANDOMLY across all carton layers — top, middle, and bottom — to ensure representative sampling.

### Q: Inspector records measurement as 52cm when actual is 52.5cm. Says 'close enough.' What error is this?
**A:** Rounding/approximation error. Inspector must record EXACT measurements to the nearest 0.5cm or as specified. '52.5' rounded to '52' can make an out-of-tolerance measurement appear within tolerance. Accurate recording is mandatory.

### Q: Inspector passes a garment where composition label is sewn upside down because label content is correct. Acceptable?
**A:** NO. Upside-down/reversed labels = OPERATIONAL packaging defect. The consumer cannot read it properly. Correct information presented incorrectly = defect. Inspector must record this as a defect.

### Q: Inspector finds factory manager constantly watching and commenting while inspector checks garments. Inspector doesn't object. Problem?
**A:** Inspector should maintain independence. Factory supervision during inspection can create bias pressure. Inspector should politely but firmly ask factory staff to allow independent checking. Interference should be documented in the report.

### Q: Inspector takes photos only of defective pieces but not of passing pieces or general production. Is this complete photo documentation?
**A:** Incomplete. Inspector should also photograph: approved sealed sample comparison, overall production view, carton stacking, hanger/label setup, and measurement process. Photos of only defects provide incomplete inspection record.

### Q: Inspector checks POM measurements while garment is laid flat on uneven table surface. Error?
**A:** YES. Garment must be measured on a FLAT, CLEAN, SMOOTH surface. Uneven surfaces cause incorrect measurements due to fabric bunching. This leads to false POM readings. Always use a proper inspection table.

### Q: Inspector reuses the same inspection result template from previous style without updating style number, PO, and date. Error?
**A:** CRITICAL administrative error. Each inspection report must be uniquely filled with correct: style number, PO number, factory name, inspection date, and inspector name. Reusing templates without updating = documentation fraud risk.

### Q: Inspector classifies a cracked zipper slider as a MINOR defect because it still opens and closes the zipper. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Cracked zipper slider = MAJOR defect regardless of current functionality. Cracked hardware will fail during consumer use. Structural integrity issues in metal/plastic components = MAJOR classification.

### Q: Inspector uses AQL Level I instead of Level II for a normal shipment. Mistake?
**A:** YES. Renner standard requires AQL Level II for normal inspections unless specifically stated otherwise. Using Level I gives a smaller sample size (less rigorous), which underrepresents defects in the production.

### Q: Inspector does not measure any footwear — only checks visual appearance. Acceptable?
**A:** NO. Footwear inspections include dimensional checks (heel height, sole thickness, toe length tolerances). Skipping measurements = incomplete inspection. All categories require relevant POM/dimensional checks per manual.

### Q: Inspector passes inspection without receiving/seeing the sealed sample, relying on memory from a previous similar style. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Every inspection requires physical comparison with the current sealed sample. Memory from previous styles is insufficient and unreliable. If no sealed sample is available, inspector must report this and consult QC office.

### Q: Inspector submits report on the same day but from a different location (not the factory) where inspection took place. Is GPS/location data important?
**A:** YES. Digital inspection systems (like Inspectorio) may capture GPS location data. Submitting reports from wrong location creates audit trail discrepancy. Inspectors should submit reports on-site or clearly document if submitted off-site with reason.

### Q: Inspector finds a defect but cannot decide if it is major or minor. Instead of escalating, inspector classifies it as minor to be safe. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. When unsure of defect classification, inspector must consult the defect code list and escalate to QC office if still uncertain. Under-classifying (major → minor) misrepresents inspection results and inflates pass rates.

### Q: Inspector checks only first 50 garments from AQL sample of 125 and extrapolates results. Mistake?
**A:** YES. Inspector must check ALL pieces in the AQL sample. Extrapolating from a subset of the sample is statistically invalid and violates AQL inspection protocol. Every sampled piece must be individually examined.

### Q: Inspector reads barcode with scanner but doesn't cross-check barcode data against purchase order. Sufficient?
**A:** INSUFFICIENT. Scanning barcodes for readability is only half the check. Inspector must verify barcode DATA matches the PO details (correct article number, size, color, country of origin). Readable but wrong data = LOGISTICS defect.

### Q: Inspector agrees to re-inspect the same 125 pieces after factory quickly fixed visible defects, instead of drawing a new random sample. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Re-inspection after rework must use a NEW random sample. Checking the same 125 pre-identified pieces after repair only validates those specific pieces, not the overall production quality. New sample = new result.

### Q: Inspector passes inspection but does not note that the sealed sample was damaged/altered. Later buyer disputes result. Who is at fault?
**A:** Inspector made an error by not documenting sealed sample condition. Any issues with the sealed sample (damage, alteration, missing sections) MUST be documented in the inspection report. This protects both the inspector and the process.

### Q: Inspector inspects 2 separate POs as one combined inspection and issues single report. Acceptable?
**A:** NO. Each PO requires its own independent inspection report. Combined reports create confusion about which PO passed/failed and make traceability impossible. Each PO = separate AQL sample + separate report.

### Q: Inspector skips checking care labels because 'they were fine last time.' Correct approach?
**A:** WRONG. Past inspection results cannot be assumed for current production. Labels can change between batches (printing errors, supplier changes). Every inspection must independently verify all mandatory points including care labels.

### Q: Inspector does not check children's garment specific safety points (VX codes) for a children's pyjama style. Mistake?
**A:** CRITICAL MISTAKE. Children's garments MUST be checked against all applicable VX safety codes. Missing safety checks on children's products can result in unsafe products reaching consumers. This is an unacceptable inspection error.

### Q: Inspector verbally tells factory 'you can ship' before submitting official report. Then report shows issues. What was wrong?
**A:** Inspector must NEVER give verbal clearance before official report is submitted. Only the formal inspection report is the official record. Verbal clearance that conflicts with the formal report creates legal and operational confusion.

### Q: Inspector measures chest width at armhole level instead of 2.5cm below armhole. Error?
**A:** YES. Measurement position matters critically. Different measurement points give different numbers. Inspector must follow EXACT measurement points as specified in the POM sheet. Incorrect measurement point = wrong data.

### Q: Inspector finds 2 types of defects but records only the more serious one to keep the report simple. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. ALL found defects must be recorded, not just the most serious. The report must document every defect type with quantity and location. Omitting defects from report = incomplete/falsified inspection record.

### Q: Inspector counts a pulled thread as 2 separate defects (1 in front, 1 in back of same garment). Correct counting?
**A:** NO. One defect per garment counts as 1 defective unit, regardless of how many defects are on that piece. Inspector counts NUMBER OF DEFECTIVE GARMENTS, not total number of defect instances (in most AQL systems).

### Q: Inspector does not photograph the master carton label during logistics check. Is this an issue?
**A:** YES. Master carton labels must be photographed as part of the inspection evidence. Label photos verify: style number, PO, size, quantity, barcode, and country of origin. Missing carton label photos = incomplete documentation.

### Q: Inspector approves a garment where only one shoulder seam has correct width but other shoulder seam is 0.5cm narrower. Decision?
**A:** Inspector should classify as defect. Asymmetrical seam widths = MAJOR operational defect (affects durability and appearance). Both seam widths must be within tolerance. Document and record as defective piece.

### Q: Inspector checks garments under fluorescent warehouse lighting only. Does not check under daylight/D65 light. Complete?
**A:** INCOMPLETE for color-critical checks. Color evaluation should be done under standard D65 illuminant (daylight simulation) for accurate shade comparison. Warehouse fluorescent lighting can mask or exaggerate color differences.

### Q: Inspector forgets to check carton drop test requirement during logistics inspection. Mistake?
**A:** YES if carton drop test is in the spec requirements. Inspector must complete ALL points in the logistics checklist. Missing required tests = incomplete inspection. Drop test verifies carton strength for transport.

### Q: Inspector checks buttons for 4-hole buttons but does not check for 2-hole button sewing pattern differences. Error?
**A:** Yes — thorough button inspection includes checking sewing pattern (thread crossing pattern, shank height, button attachment tightness) not just presence/count. Different sewing patterns affect durability. Inspector must follow accessory checklist fully.

### Q: Inspector finds embroidery on front but does not check if embroidery has required backing/stabilizer. Miss?
**A:** YES. Embroidery backing/stabilizer is a quality requirement. Missing stabilizer causes puckering, distortion, and fabric weakening. Inspector must check reverse side of embroidery for proper backing. This is part of construction inspection.

### Q: Inspector records inspection date as the date of report writing, not the actual inspection date. Error?
**A:** YES. Inspection date must be the ACTUAL date inspection was conducted at the factory. Report writing date may differ. False dates on inspection records = documentation integrity failure.

### Q: Inspector passes inspection but fails to note that the sample was 10% below AQL quantity (e.g., 113 instead of 125). Problem?
**A:** YES. Inspector must note ANY deviation from required AQL sample size. If full sample size was not achievable, reason must be documented. Results based on sub-standard sample size may be challenged.

### Q: Inspector finds 1 piece in 125 with a critical defect. Instead of REJECT, marks it as 'isolated' and passes. Acceptable?
**A:** NO. For critical defects, AQL acceptance number = 0. Even 1 critical defect = FAIL/REJECT. There is no 'isolated critical' exception. This is a fundamental AQL rule violation.

### Q: Inspector checks hangers are present but does not verify they are the correct Renner-specified hanger type and code. Complete?
**A:** INCOMPLETE. Presence of hangers is not sufficient. Inspector must verify the correct RENNER-SPECIFIED hanger type is used. Wrong hanger type = packaging defect even if hangers are physically present.

### Q: Inspector sends inspection result to factory before sending to QC office. Correct order?
**A:** WRONG ORDER. Inspection results go to QC OFFICE first. QC office communicates with factory officially. Inspector should never communicate results directly to factory before office review, as this bypasses official communication channels.

### Q: Inspector does not check footwear soles for correct embossed/printed branding that should be on the outsole. Miss?
**A:** YES. Footwear outsole branding/marking is a product specification point. Inspector must verify outsole markings match the approved sample. Missing or wrong outsole markings = MAJOR branding defect.

### Q: Inspector measures inside leg length without straightening the garment first. Error?
**A:** YES. Garment must be laid FLAT and STRAIGHT before measuring inside leg length. Twisted or angled position gives inaccurate measurements. This is a basic measurement technique error.

### Q: Inspector classifies all defects found in a small accessory (jewelry) using garment defect codes. Correct?
**A:** NO. Accessories, footwear, and jewelry have their own specific defect classification systems. Using garment defect codes for jewelry = incorrect classification. Inspector must use the relevant product category defect code list.

### Q: Inspector does not check if polybag has required ventilation holes for children's products. Miss?
**A:** CRITICAL MISS. Polybags for children's products MUST have ventilation holes (safety requirement to prevent suffocation risk). Missing ventilation holes = CRITICAL safety defect. Inspector must verify this for all children's products.

### Q: Inspector submits PASS result but later realizes they forgot to check the RFID validation. Can inspector amend the report?
**A:** Inspector must immediately contact QC office and report the oversight. Amending official reports requires formal process. A re-inspection may be required for RFID validation. Inspector cannot self-amend a submitted report without office approval.

### Q: Inspector leaves the factory before counting and verifying total carton count against packing list. Acceptable?
**A:** NO. Carton count verification against packing list is a mandatory logistics check. Inspector must complete this before leaving factory. Leaving early without completing all checklist items = incomplete inspection.

### Q: Inspector measures hip width with the waistband folded over instead of flat. Error?
**A:** YES. Waistband position during measurement must match the specified measurement method. Folded waistband changes garment position and gives different hip measurements. Follow exact garment positioning instructions in the POM guide.

### Q: Inspector finds 3 different versions of the same style in production. Only 1 version matches the spec. Inspector checks only the matching version. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Inspector must document ALL versions found. The presence of non-matching versions = MAJOR conformity issue. Inspector must flag all variants to QC office. Checking only the correct version hides the mix-version production problem.

### Q: Inspector does not verify if the factory has the correct Renner-approved label stock (correct Pantone/font/layout). Error?
**A:** YES. Label stock verification is part of factory audit/PPM responsibilities. During inspection, inspector must confirm labels match the approved spec. Wrong label stock = packaging defect even if information on label is correct.

### Q: Inspector finds defect percentage is borderline. Inspector asks factory manager 'what do you think?' before making decision. Correct?
**A:** NO. The inspection decision is INSPECTOR'S responsibility based on AQL standards and manual rules. Consulting factory manager compromises inspector independence. Inspector should consult QC OFFICE if uncertain, not the factory.

### Q: Inspector checks 125 pcs but counts only 118 in the report. Error?
**A:** YES. Report must accurately reflect actual sample size checked. Reporting wrong sample size (either higher or lower than actual) = documentation error. Always record exact number checked.

### Q: Inspector assumes that because the factory passed last inspection, current inspection will also pass and shortcuts the process. Correct?
**A:** WRONG. Past inspection results have NO bearing on current inspection. Every inspection is independent. Factory performance can vary batch to batch. Inspector must conduct full inspection every time regardless of history.

### Q: Inspector measures T-shirt chest at 1cm below armhole instead of correct 2.5cm. How much difference can this make?
**A:** Significant. Measuring at 1cm vs 2.5cm below armhole captures different chest widths due to garment shaping. This can mean 1-2cm difference which might falsely show a measurement within or outside tolerance. Always use exact specified measurement point.

### Q: Inspector takes 10 photos and deletes blurry ones, ending up with 3 photos. Is 3 photos sufficient for a full inspection?
**A:** Generally insufficient. A complete inspection photo package should include: sealed sample comparison, overall garment front/back, defect close-ups, measurement process, carton/label, hanger setup, RFID scan screen. 3 photos rarely covers this.

### Q: Inspector is sick but conducts inspection anyway without informing QC office. If errors occur in report, what is the concern?
**A:** Inspector should inform QC office if health affects capability to conduct thorough inspection. Conducting inspection while unwell risks inspection errors. QC office can arrange replacement or reschedule. Always prioritize inspection quality.

### Q: Inspector finds a critical defect in piece #87 of 125 but misrecords it as piece #78. Does the number matter?
**A:** Piece numbers in documentation should be consistent and accurate. If piece-specific records are used for re-inspection traceability, wrong numbers create confusion. Document all findings accurately including piece identification.

### Q: Inspector does not check if factory has correct INMETRO certification for electrical accessories (if applicable). Oversight?
**A:** YES. For products requiring regulatory certifications (INMETRO, ABNT, etc.), inspector must verify certification presence and validity where required by buyer spec. Missing mandatory certifications = CRITICAL compliance defect.

### Q: Inspector passes inspection but fails to document that production quantity was less than 80% of PO quantity. What was missed?
**A:** Inspector must document production quantity vs PO quantity. If less than 80% is ready, inspection should not start (or at minimum this must be documented). This affects shipment scheduling and buyer planning. It is a mandatory report field.

## Topic: INSPECTORIO-CAPA (50 Questions)

### Q: What does CAPA stand for in QC context?
**A:** CAPA = Corrective Action and Preventive Action. It is a systematic process to: (C) identify and CORRECT an existing defect/problem, and (P) take PREVENTIVE actions to ensure the same problem does not recur in future production.

### Q: When must a CAPA be issued for a Renner inspection?
**A:** A CAPA must be issued when: inspection result is HOLD or REJECT, critical defects are found, AQL limits are exceeded, any of the four pillars (quality/POM/RFID/logistics) fails, and when there is a systematic defect pattern even below AQL threshold.

### Q: What are the 5 key components of a complete CAPA report?
**A:** 1. PROBLEM DESCRIPTION: exact defect with photos and quantities. 2. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS: why the defect occurred. 3. IMMEDIATE CORRECTIVE ACTION: what is done RIGHT NOW to fix current production. 4. PREVENTIVE ACTION: what will change to prevent recurrence. 5. TIMELINE: deadline for completion and verification.

### Q: In Inspectorio, how is a CAPA linked to an inspection report?
**A:** In Inspectorio, a CAPA is created directly from the inspection report. Inspector flags specific defect findings. The CAPA module allows attaching photos, assigning to factory, setting deadlines, and tracking factory responses. All CAPA data is linked to the original inspection report ID.

### Q: What is the typical response timeline for a factory to submit CAPA after a HOLD result?
**A:** Standard CAPA response deadline: 24-72 hours for initial response, with full corrective action plan submitted within 5 business days. Exact deadlines depend on Renner/QC office requirements. Factory must acknowledge receipt of CAPA within 24 hours.

### Q: What are the two types of root causes in CAPA analysis?
**A:** 1. IMMEDIATE ROOT CAUSE: the direct cause of the defect (e.g., wrong needle gauge caused fabric damage). 2. SYSTEMIC ROOT CAUSE: the underlying process failure that allowed the immediate cause to occur (e.g., no needle change protocol in place = system failure).

### Q: Factory submits CAPA saying 'workers were trained again.' Is this sufficient root cause and corrective action?
**A:** NO. 'Retraining workers' alone is NOT sufficient CAPA. It does not address: WHY training failed in the first place, what specific training was lacking, how will training be verified effective, and what systemic change prevents recurrence. CAPA must be specific and measurable.

### Q: What is the '5 Why' method and how is it used in CAPA?
**A:** The 5 Why method: ask 'Why did this happen?' 5 times consecutively to reach the ROOT cause. Example: Broken stitches → Why? Thread tension wrong → Why? Machine not calibrated → Why? No calibration schedule → Why? No maintenance SOP → Why? No quality system documented. SOLUTION: Create calibration SOP.

### Q: A factory's CAPA for wrong label says: 'We already sorted all wrong labels and replaced with correct ones.' Is the CAPA complete?
**A:** INCOMPLETE. The corrective action (replacing labels) addresses the current batch. But the CAPA is missing: root cause analysis (how wrong labels entered production), preventive action (what process change prevents recurrence), and verification method (how will this be checked going forward).

### Q: How should a QC manager verify that a factory's CAPA has been effectively implemented?
**A:** Verification methods: 1. Request photographic evidence of corrective action. 2. Schedule a re-inspection or follow-up visit. 3. Check next shipment for absence of same defect. 4. Request updated SOPs or work instruction documents. 5. Review factory's internal QC records for the corrected process.

### Q: What is a 'containment action' in CAPA and when is it used?
**A:** Containment action is the IMMEDIATE step to prevent defective products from reaching the buyer/consumer BEFORE root cause is found. Example: Hold all production, 100% check current batch, quarantine affected units. It is the first step in CAPA before root cause analysis.

### Q: Factory CAPA for AQL failure on stitching defects proposes: 'Increase QC inspection frequency on sewing line.' Is this a preventive action?
**A:** PARTIAL. Increasing inspection frequency is a DETECTION improvement (catches more defects), not a true PREVENTIVE action. True prevention requires fixing the root cause (e.g., machine calibration, thread quality, operator training). Both detection AND prevention improvements should be in CAPA.

### Q: How does a CAPA differ from a re-inspection?
**A:** CAPA = a management process to identify causes and implement fixes (happens at factory level). RE-INSPECTION = a new inspection conducted by QC inspector after factory claims to have fixed the issues (verification step). CAPA is the factory's internal action; re-inspection is the QC verification of that action.

### Q: What information must a QC inspector include when raising a CAPA in Inspectorio?
**A:** Inspector must include: defect code/name, defect quantity found, affected style/PO/SKU, clear photo evidence (minimum 3 photos per defect type), severity classification, exact location on garment, production batch details, and recommended corrective action if known.

### Q: Factory submits CAPA with photos showing defective pieces sorted out. Inspector visits for re-inspection and finds same defects still occurring. What does this mean?
**A:** This means the CAPA was only a containment action (sorting) without true root cause fix. The corrective action was insufficient. The factory failed to address the process failure causing the defect. Re-inspection result: FAIL. Escalated CAPA required with stronger action plan.

### Q: What is 'CAPA effectiveness verification' and who is responsible for it?
**A:** CAPA effectiveness verification = confirming that the implemented corrective actions have actually eliminated the defect. Responsibility: QC Manager reviews CAPA response, schedules re-inspection, and formally closes the CAPA only when verified that same defect is absent in subsequent production.

### Q: How many days does a factory typically have to complete corrective action and request re-inspection?
**A:** Typically 5-10 business days for corrective action completion, though this depends on: severity of the defect, shipment deadline pressure, type of defect (rework vs remake), and QC office policy. Factory must not ship before re-inspection is passed.

### Q: What is a 'recurring CAPA' and why is it significant?
**A:** A recurring CAPA occurs when the same defect type reappears in subsequent inspections despite previous CAPA closure. This indicates: root cause was not properly identified, corrective actions were temporary, or systemic process failure is deeper than initially thought. Recurring CAPAs trigger escalation (factory warning, audit, or order reduction).

### Q: For a POM failure CAPA, what specific factory process documents should be requested as part of the response?
**A:** Request: 1. Updated POM measurement guide for factory QC team. 2. Corrected pattern or grading documents if pattern was wrong. 3. Measurement records from factory's own QC after correction. 4. Training record showing sewing operators were re-briefed on measurement points. 5. Evidence of pattern correction if needed.

### Q: What is the difference between 'corrective action' and 'preventive action' in the CAPA framework?
**A:** CORRECTIVE ACTION: reactive — fixes the CURRENT problem (e.g., rework 500 defective pieces). PREVENTIVE ACTION: proactive — changes the PROCESS to prevent future occurrence (e.g., install 100% automated stitch inspection system on production line). Both are required in a complete CAPA.

### Q: Factory CAPA for broken snaps states 'We changed the snap supplier.' Is this a complete CAPA?
**A:** PARTIALLY COMPLETE. Changing supplier = corrective action for the current problem. But CAPA needs: why the original supplier's snaps failed (were they defective or misused?), incoming QC protocol for new supplier snaps, and how factory will test snaps going forward. Add preventive actions.

### Q: At what stage of production should a 'Preventive CAPA' be triggered versus a reactive CAPA?
**A:** Preventive CAPA is triggered BEFORE a defect reaches AQL failure level — proactive response to: inline inspection showing upward trend in a defect type, pattern of minor defects that could become major, PPM observations about risky production setups, or known supplier quality issues. Reactive CAPA is triggered AFTER AQL failure.

### Q: What is a 'CAPA deadline breach' and what happens when factory misses the deadline?
**A:** CAPA deadline breach = factory fails to submit corrective action or complete rework within the agreed timeline. Consequences: Renner/QC office may escalate the case, the PO may be at risk of cancellation, factory's supplier score/rating is negatively impacted, and QC office may conduct unannounced follow-up inspection.

### Q: How should a QC Manager structure a CAPA email to a factory after a HOLD result?
**A:** CAPA email should include: 1. Reference to inspection report number and date. 2. Specific defects found with photos attached. 3. Quantity affected. 4. Request for root cause analysis. 5. Required: corrective and preventive action plan. 6. Deadline for response. 7. Confirmation that no shipment until re-inspection passed.

### Q: Can a factory ship goods while a CAPA is still open?
**A:** NO. Standard practice: NO SHIPMENT while CAPA is open and re-inspection not yet passed. Shipping before CAPA resolution risks defective goods reaching Renner stores. Exception: only if buyer/QC office formally approves shipment with noted risk and factory's remediation plan accepted.

### Q: What is a 'Fishbone Diagram' (Ishikawa) and how does it support CAPA?
**A:** Fishbone diagram is a root cause analysis tool showing all possible causes of a problem organized into categories (Machine, Method, Man, Material, Measurement, Environment). Used in CAPA to visually map all contributing factors. Helps factory conduct systematic root cause analysis instead of guessing.

### Q: Factory resolves a barcode CAPA by reprinting all labels. Three months later, same wrong barcode issue recurs. What systemic issue does this indicate?
**A:** Recurrence indicates the factory did NOT fix the root cause. The root cause was likely a data entry or setup error in their barcode printing system. The CAPA only fixed symptoms (wrong labels) but not cause (wrong data in system). Factory needs to implement a barcode data verification protocol at start of every production run.

### Q: What information should be in the 'problem description' section of a CAPA report?
**A:** Problem description must include: exact defect name and code, quantity found defective (out of sample size), defect location on product, severity classification (critical/major/minor), inspection date and report reference, and clear photos showing the defect clearly.

### Q: What is a 'Type 1' vs 'Type 2' CAPA error in quality management?
**A:** Type 1: Over-reacting — issuing CAPA for naturally occurring variation within acceptable limits (false alarm). Type 2: Under-reacting — failing to issue CAPA when a real systematic defect exists. Both errors are costly. QC Manager must calibrate CAPA triggers carefully to avoid both.

### Q: After CAPA is completed and re-inspection passed, what is the final step?
**A:** Final step: CAPA CLOSURE. QC Manager formally closes the CAPA in Inspectorio system, records: date of closure, verification method used, re-inspection result reference, and any notes on effectiveness. Closed CAPA becomes part of factory's quality performance history.

### Q: For a labeling CAPA, what documents must the factory provide to prove the issue is fixed?
**A:** Factory must provide: updated label artwork with correct information, approval sign-off on new label from QC/buyer, photos of new labels on production units, updated label specification checklist used by factory QC, and disposal/destruction evidence of wrong labels.

### Q: What is the importance of CAPA trending in QC management?
**A:** CAPA trending = analyzing patterns in CAPA data over time. Useful for: identifying factories with recurring defect types, spotting seasonal quality dips, evaluating supplier improvement, targeting training needs, and making strategic decisions about factory relationships. Trending turns individual CAPAs into strategic quality intelligence.

### Q: Factory's CAPA for pilling defect states: 'We increased yarn twist in production.' Is this sufficient?
**A:** PARTIALLY SUFFICIENT. Increasing yarn twist = corrective action targeting one possible cause. Complete CAPA needs: confirmation that yarn twist was confirmed as the root cause (through lab testing), evidence that new twist level meets spec, test results from new production showing acceptable pilling rating, and QC protocol for ongoing pilling verification.

### Q: In CAPA language, what does 'rework' mean vs 'remake'?
**A:** REWORK = correcting defective products that can be fixed without remaking (e.g., re-sewing a seam, replacing a button, re-pressing a garment). REMAKE = defects cannot be fixed; the piece must be completely remade from new materials. Remake is more costly and requires more time. CAPA should specify which action is being taken.

### Q: What is an 'escape' in CAPA terminology?
**A:** An 'escape' is a defect that passed through the factory's own internal QC and was only caught by the external QC inspection. Escapes indicate weak factory internal quality control. CAPA for escapes must address WHY the defect was not caught internally, not just fix the defect.

### Q: How should QC Manager handle a CAPA where factory disagrees with the defect classification?
**A:** If factory disputes defect classification: 1. Review evidence (photos, measurements). 2. Reference the defect manual for correct classification criteria. 3. If still disputed, escalate to buyer/QC director. 4. Document the dispute in the CAPA. 5. Resolution must be based on manual standards, not opinion. If manual is ambiguous, buyer interpretation is final.

### Q: What is a 'CAPA audit' and when does it happen?
**A:** A CAPA audit is a formal review of all open and recently closed CAPAs for a factory. It evaluates: response timeliness, root cause analysis quality, corrective action effectiveness, and recurrence rate. Typically conducted during factory audits or quarterly supplier reviews. Helps assess factory's quality management maturity.

### Q: For a children's garment safety CAPA (VX defect), what is the escalation level?
**A:** Children's garment VX safety defect CAPA = HIGHEST priority escalation. Goes directly to QC Director and Buyer. Production must STOP for the affected style. 100% inspection of all produced units required. Design/pattern change must be approved by buyer before production resumes. Timeline is compressed due to safety risk.

### Q: What does 'out-of-control process' mean in CAPA context?
**A:** Out-of-control process = a production process where defects occur randomly and unpredictably, exceeding statistical control limits. This indicates the process is fundamentally unstable. CAPA for out-of-control process must include process capability study (Cpk analysis), machine re-calibration, and process redesign.

### Q: Factory CAPA includes a photo of the defective pieces put in a separate bin. What does this photo confirm?
**A:** The photo confirms CONTAINMENT — that identified defective pieces have been isolated from conforming production. This is the first step but does NOT confirm root cause fix or preventive action. Additional photos needed: rework/reworked pieces, updated work instruction, measurement records.

### Q: What is 'CAPA overload' and how does a QC Manager prevent it?
**A:** CAPA overload = too many CAPAs open simultaneously, causing factory to become overwhelmed and unable to address any effectively. Prevention: prioritize CAPAs by severity (critical > major > minor), set realistic timelines, focus factory on root causes not just rework, and schedule regular CAPA review meetings.

### Q: For a measurement (POM) CAPA, what three things must the factory demonstrate?
**A:** Factory must demonstrate: 1. CAUSE identified — was it wrong pattern, operator error, or wrong measurement method? 2. CORRECTION done — pattern adjusted, operator retrained, correct measurement tools provided. 3. VERIFICATION — new measurement records showing corrected measurements within tolerance on production pieces.

### Q: What is the relationship between CAPA and the Inspectorio platform for Renner suppliers?
**A:** Inspectorio is the digital platform where Renner QC processes are managed. CAPAs raised in Inspectorio are linked to inspection reports, tracked with deadlines, factory responses are logged with evidence uploads, and QC managers can monitor all open/closed CAPAs in real-time. It creates a transparent, traceable CAPA history.

### Q: What information must a re-inspection report contain when closing a CAPA?
**A:** Re-inspection report for CAPA closure must include: reference to original inspection report and CAPA number, new AQL sample results, specific check on the previously failed defect points, comparison with CAPA corrective actions taken, photographic evidence, and final result (PASS/FAIL) with inspector signature.

### Q: Can a CAPA be closed without re-inspection?
**A:** Generally NO for major defects or AQL failures. Re-inspection is the standard verification method. Exception: for minor administrative issues (e.g., incorrect carton marking) where photo evidence clearly shows correction and buyer/QC office accepts evidence-based closure without re-inspection. All closure decisions require QC Manager authorization.

### Q: What is a 'CAPA action plan matrix' and how does it help factories?
**A:** A CAPA action plan matrix is a structured table showing: defect | root cause | corrective action | responsible person | target date | verification method. It gives factories a clear action plan format and prevents vague responses. It also makes it easy for QC to track completion of each specific action.

### Q: What is the difference between a CAPA for a 'sporadic' defect vs a 'systematic' defect?
**A:** SPORADIC: random occurrence, not consistent pattern. Root cause often: operator error, material batch issue, or one-time machine problem. Fix: specific targeted action. SYSTEMATIC: same defect repeating across pieces/lots. Root cause: process or design failure. Fix: process redesign, SOP update, equipment overhaul.

### Q: Factory submits CAPA but photos are unclear and blurry. Should QC Manager accept?
**A:** NO. CAPA evidence must be clear, properly lit, and show the specific corrected point. Blurry photos cannot be used as verification evidence. QC Manager should reject the CAPA submission and request clear re-submission. 'No clear evidence = no CAPA accepted.'

### Q: What happens if a factory refuses to submit a CAPA or does not acknowledge a HOLD result?
**A:** Factory non-compliance with CAPA: 1. QC office escalates to procurement/sourcing team. 2. Factory is flagged as non-compliant in supplier database. 3. Renner may cancel or reduce the order. 4. Factory may be placed on 'probation' or 'audit required' status. Non-response to CAPA is taken very seriously.

### Q: What role does the QC Manager play in CAPA compared to the inspector's role?
**A:** INSPECTOR: identifies defects, documents findings, raises initial CAPA flag in Inspectorio. QC MANAGER: reviews CAPA, evaluates root cause quality, approves or rejects factory's corrective action plan, schedules re-inspection, tracks CAPA completion, and formally closes CAPA. Manager is the decision-maker; inspector is the data gatherer.

## Topic: MIXED-REVIEW (80 Questions)

### Q: What are Renner's Four Pillars of Inspection?
**A:** 1. AQL QUALITY: defect-level check using statistical sampling. 2. POM MEASUREMENTS: dimensional conformance to spec. 3. RFID/BARCODES: logistics technology verification. 4. LOGISTICS/PACKAGING: carton, hanger, polybag, label checks. ALL four must pass for inspection to be approved.

### Q: What is AQL and what does 'AQL 2.5' mean for major defects?
**A:** AQL = Acceptable Quality Limit. It is the maximum defect percentage considered acceptable. AQL 2.5 for majors means: in a lot with 2.5% or fewer major defects, the lot is considered acceptable. In practice it defines the acceptance number (Ac) for a given sample size at that defect level.

### Q: For a lot of 1,200 pieces with AQL Level II, what is the sample size?
**A:** Lot 1,201-3,200 → Sample size letter = K → Sample size = 125 pieces per ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 AQL Level II table. Acceptance numbers: Critical = 0, Major (AQL 2.5) = 7, Minor (AQL 4.0) = 10.

### Q: What is the definition of a 'critical defect' in Renner inspection?
**A:** Critical defect: a defect that poses a safety risk to the consumer, violates regulatory requirements, or causes a product to be completely unusable. AQL acceptance = 0. Examples: exposed sharp metal, missing safety labeling on children's garments, embedded needles, toxic substance non-compliance.

### Q: What is the difference between Inline Inspection (ILI) and Final Random Inspection (FRI)?
**A:** INLINE (ILI): done at 20-30% of production to catch defects early while production can still be corrected. FRI (Final): done when 80-100% production is complete and packed, evaluating the finished goods before shipment. Both use AQL sampling.

### Q: What should an inspector do if production is only 70% complete when they arrive for FRI?
**A:** Inspector should NOT start FRI. Minimum 80% of production must be complete for FRI. Inspector documents the situation, informs QC office, and reschedules. Factory is responsible for ensuring readiness before calling for inspection.

### Q: What is a sealed sample and what is its purpose in inspection?
**A:** A sealed sample is a pre-approved product sample signed/sealed by the buyer and factory as the official reference standard. Its purpose: provides the exact reference for color, construction, trimming, measurement, and finish that bulk production must match.

### Q: What is RFID technology and why does Renner use it?
**A:** RFID = Radio Frequency Identification. Renner uses RFID tags on all products to enable: automated inventory management, faster checkout, anti-theft protection (EAS function), stock accuracy in stores, and supply chain tracking. Each RFID tag is programmed with unique product data.

### Q: What defect code prefix is used for safety defects in children's garments?
**A:** VX prefix (e.g., VX3, VX7, VX11). These codes indicate children's garment safety violations based on EN 14682, ISO 13688, and Brazilian ABNT standards. VX defects = CRITICAL, zero acceptance.

### Q: What is the maximum drawstring length at waist level for children's garments (0-14 years)?
**A:** Maximum free-hanging drawstring/cord length at waist level = 14cm. At hood/neck area = strictly controlled (no functional drawstrings at hood for 0-7 years; specific maximum for 7-14). Inspector must measure with garment laid flat, drawstring fully extended.

### Q: How are defects classified in Renner inspection system?
**A:** Three levels: CRITICAL = safety/regulatory risk, AQL = 0. MAJOR = significantly affects appearance, function, or consumer satisfaction, AQL = 2.5. MINOR = slight imperfection, not significantly affecting function, AQL = 4.0.

### Q: What information appears on a Renner master carton label?
**A:** Master carton label includes: Renner article number, PO number, style/reference, color, size assortment, quantity, country of origin, gross/net weight, box dimensions, barcode (EAN/ITF-14 or GS1), destination warehouse, and any required compliance markings.

### Q: For AQL Level II with lot size 501-1200, what is the sample size?
**A:** Lot 501-1200 → Sample size letter = J → Sample size = 80 pieces. Acceptance numbers at AQL 2.5 for majors: Ac = 5. Rejection number: Re = 6.

### Q: What is Inspectorio and how does the QC team use it for Renner inspections?
**A:** Inspectorio is the digital quality management platform used for Renner inspections. Functions: schedule inspections, conduct inspections on mobile/tablet, upload photos, record defects, calculate AQL results, submit reports digitally, manage CAPA, and track factory performance history.

### Q: What is PLM and how does it support QC inspections?
**A:** PLM = Product Lifecycle Management system. Used for Renner to manage: product specifications, tech packs, approved styles, and approved samples. Inspectors check PLM for the latest approved spec before inspection. Ensures inspector always uses current approved specifications.

### Q: What is NPF and what does it contain relevant to inspection?
**A:** NPF = Nota de Produto Fornecedor (Supplier Product Note). Contains specific supplier/style requirements that supplement the general inspection manual. QC must check NPF for any style-specific deviations or additional requirements before conducting inspection.

### Q: For footwear AQL inspection, what sample size would be used for 800 pairs?
**A:** 800 pairs: lot 501-1200 → sample size 80 pairs (Level II). For footwear, each 'pair' is one unit. Sample 80 individual pairs. AQL acceptance: Critical = 0, Major AQL 2.5 = Ac 5, Minor AQL 4.0 = Ac 7.

### Q: What is the purpose of a Pre-Production Meeting (PPM) in the Renner supply chain?
**A:** PPM = a meeting held before bulk production starts between factory technical team and QC/buyer. Purpose: review critical quality requirements, confirm sealed sample, clarify specifications, address potential production challenges, align on inspection schedule, and confirm trim/label approvals. Prevents defects through proactive communication.

### Q: What does 'ITF-14 barcode' represent on Renner master cartons?
**A:** ITF-14 (Interleaved 2 of 5) is the standard barcode format used on master carton labels for logistics/warehouse scanning. It encodes the GTIN-14 (Global Trade Item Number) identifying the product contents. Inspector must verify ITF-14 is scannable and data correct.

### Q: How many hang tags/price tickets must typically be on a Renner garment?
**A:** Standard: 1 main Renner price tag (with barcode, price, article number) plus any required regulatory labels. Some styles require additional care cards. Check spec sheet for exact hang tag requirement per style. Multiple tags must be correctly positioned per approved sample.

### Q: What is the color shade evaluation method for Renner bulk production?
**A:** Shade evaluation: compare bulk fabric/garment under D65 daylight simulator light source. Compare against sealed sample. Evaluate for: hue match, depth (light/dark), tone (warm/cool). Rating typically 1-5 (5 = excellent match). Minimum acceptable: Grade 3. Any deviation below Grade 3 = HOLD.

### Q: What is 'lot-to-lot shade variation' and why is it a concern for Renner?
**A:** Lot-to-lot shade variation = color differences between different dye lots or production batches of the same style. Problem: if different lots are sent to same store, garments on shelf will look different. Renner requires all pieces in same PO to be from same fabric lot or within acceptable shade tolerance.

### Q: For a children's garment with both VX defect and AQL major defects exceeding limit — what is the result?
**A:** IMMEDIATE REJECT on two counts: (1) VX safety defect = zero tolerance, automatic reject regardless of AQL. (2) AQL major excess = independent reject. Two separate CAPA items required. Production must be stopped and reviewed.

### Q: What is the difference between 'acceptance number' (Ac) and 'rejection number' (Re) in AQL?
**A:** Ac (Acceptance Number): if found defects ≤ Ac, the lot is ACCEPTED. Re (Rejection Number): if found defects ≥ Re, the lot is REJECTED. Note: Re = Ac + 1 (they are always consecutive numbers). There is no 'middle ground' in AQL decision.

### Q: What information must be verified on the garment composition/care label?
**A:** Care label must show: fiber composition (% of each fiber in correct order by weight), care symbols (wash, dry, iron, bleach, dry clean), country of origin, and any required compliance markings (INMETRO, CA number, etc.). Label must be in correct language for market destination.

### Q: What is 'crocking' and how is it tested for Renner products?
**A:** Crocking = color transfer from fabric to other surfaces (skin, other fabrics). Tested by AATCC 8 (dry crocking) and AATCC 116 (wet crocking) methods. Rating 1-5 (5 = no transfer). Minimum acceptable: Grade 3 for dry, Grade 2-3 for wet depending on product. Crocking failure = lab reject.

### Q: What is the stitch density requirement for Renner children's garment seams?
**A:** Renner children's garments: minimum 12-14 SPI for main seams. Critical seams (shoulder, armhole, crotch) may require higher density. Lower stitch density = weaker seams = safety/durability risk for children. Inspector must physically count stitches per inch on sampled pieces.

### Q: What is the function of the 'gray scale' in QC?
**A:** Gray scale is a standardized tool for evaluating color change (after wash/treatment) and color staining (transfer to adjacent fabric). Ratings 1-5: Grade 5 = no change/no staining (best). Grade 1 = severe change/staining (worst). Renner minimum: typically Grade 3 for most applications.

### Q: For a lot of 350 pieces, AQL Level II — what is the sample size?
**A:** Lot 281-500 → Sample size letter = H → Sample size = 50 pieces. Acceptance numbers: Critical = 0, Major AQL 2.5 = Ac 3, Minor AQL 4.0 = Ac 5.

### Q: What is 'puckering' in garment construction and how is it classified?
**A:** Puckering = gathered or wave-like distortion of seams or fabric due to: wrong stitch tension, needle damage, differential fabric feed, or wrong thread type. Classification: slight puckering that lies flat after pressing = MINOR. Puckering that remains visible after pressing = MAJOR. Structural puckering causing shape distortion = MAJOR to CRITICAL.

### Q: What is a 'bar tack' and where are they required?
**A:** Bar tack = dense reinforcement stitching (typically 1-2cm wide) applied at stress concentration points. Required at: pocket openings, belt loop attachments, zipper ends, strap attachments, and any point subjected to pulling force. Missing bar tacks = MAJOR construction defect.

### Q: What is the Renner policy on RFID and EAS (anti-theft)?
**A:** Renner requires integrated RFID+EAS tags on products. The RFID tag serves dual function: inventory tracking AND electronic article surveillance (anti-theft). Inspector must verify: tag present, correctly positioned, programmed with correct data, and EAS function not already deactivated.

### Q: How is 'skip lot' inspection different from standard AQL inspection?
**A:** Skip lot = reduced inspection frequency for consistently high-performing factories (e.g., inspect every 3rd or 5th shipment instead of every shipment). Still uses AQL when inspection occurs. Not commonly applied in Renner until factory has demonstrated sustained quality performance. Standard policy = inspect every lot.

### Q: What is 'spirality' or 'torque' in knitwear and how is it detected?
**A:** Spirality = rotational twisting of knit fabric causing seams to shift from their correct position (side seam twisting to front). Detection: lay garment flat, check if side seam lies straight or twists toward front. Tolerance: typically <5% seam deviation. Excess spirality = MAJOR defect.

### Q: What are the standard AQL levels Renner uses for Critical, Major, and Minor defects?
**A:** Renner standard: CRITICAL = AQL 0 (zero tolerance). MAJOR = AQL 2.5. MINOR = AQL 4.0. These apply to AQL Level II normal inspection. Tightened inspection (after failures) may use stricter limits.

### Q: What is 'print registration' and why does it matter?
**A:** Print registration = precise alignment of different color layers in a multi-color print. Poor registration = colors misaligned (blurry edges, color bleeding between design elements). Good registration = each color layer exactly aligned. Misregistration >2mm = MAJOR visual defect for Renner.

### Q: For Renner accessories (jewelry), what chemical safety standards are most critical?
**A:** Key chemical standards: Lead (Pb) content ≤ 0.006% (60 ppm) for surface coatings; Lead ≤ 0.05% for substrate. Cadmium (Cd) ≤ 0.01% (100 ppm). Nickel (Ni) release ≤ 0.5 μg/cm²/week for skin-contact items. Compliance verified by lab test certificate.

### Q: How does the Renner logistics inspection address carton stacking strength?
**A:** Carton stacking check: inspector verifies cartons are properly packed, sealed, and can bear stacking weight during transport/storage. Some specs include carton drop test and compression test requirements. Inspector checks if carton spec matches materials used. Weak cartons = logistics defect.

### Q: What is the correct action if factory runs out of the correct Renner hangers midway through inspection?
**A:** HOLD. Inspector must note that portion of production does not have correct hangers. Factory cannot substitute different hangers. Hanger type is a packaging specification. Result: HOLD for units without correct hangers. Factory must source correct hangers before passing.

### Q: What is 'right-hand twill' vs 'left-hand twill' in denim and why might it matter for inspection?
**A:** Right-hand twill: diagonal lines run from lower-left to upper-right (↗). Left-hand twill: diagonal lines run from lower-right to upper-left (↖). Using wrong twill direction vs sealed sample = MAJOR fabric construction defect. Inspector can verify twill direction by examining the fabric weave pattern.

### Q: For Renner children's footwear, what additional VX-equivalent safety checks apply?
**A:** Children's footwear safety checks: no sharp edges on buckles/closures, no loose/small decorative elements that could detach (choking hazard for under 3 years), heel height limits per age group, toe box protection, non-slip sole requirement, and absence of allergy-risk materials in skin-contact areas.

### Q: What does 'AQL tightened inspection' mean and when is it triggered?
**A:** Tightened inspection = stricter inspection applied after factory fails consecutive shipments. Sample size increases or acceptance number decreases (stricter limits). For example: normal AQL 2.5 major limit might tighten to AQL 1.0. Triggered by: 2+ consecutive failures on same defect type.

### Q: What is the correct position for Renner's price tag (etiqueta) on a hanging garment?
**A:** Price tag position: attached to main label or seam loop, positioned so it hangs visibly at front-right of garment when on hanger. Exact position may vary by product category. Inspector verifies: tag present, correct style, readable, not covering care label, and properly attached (not loose/falling).

### Q: What is the significance of the 'seam allowance' for inspector during final inspection?
**A:** Seam allowance = the fabric margin between the stitch line and fabric edge. Insufficient seam allowance = seam may fray and break during use. Inspector checks seams at stress points for adequate allowance (typically minimum 1cm for main seams). Exposed frayed seam = MAJOR defect.

### Q: For a Renner inspection with 4 defects found — 1 critical, 3 major, 2 minor — in 125 pieces. What is the result?
**A:** 1 critical found. Critical AQL = 0. Rejection number for critical = 1. REJECT on criticals alone. The 3 majors and 2 minors are within limits (Ac for major = 7, Ac for minor = 10) but they don't matter — 1 critical = automatic REJECT.

### Q: What is 'wash shrinkage' and what tolerance does Renner apply?
**A:** Wash shrinkage = dimensional change (length/width reduction) after washing per care instructions. Tolerance varies by fabric type: typically ±3-5% for knits, ±3% for wovens. Excess shrinkage means garment will not fit correctly after first wash. Lab test is required for shrinkage compliance.

### Q: What is the role of the 'Sealed Sample' vs the 'Counter Sample' in Renner QC?
**A:** SEALED SAMPLE: official approved standard, physically signed/sealed by buyer. COUNTER SAMPLE: copy of sealed sample kept by factory for production reference. Counter sample should match sealed sample. Inspector uses the SEALED sample (not counter sample) as the definitive standard for comparison during inspection.

### Q: How should inspector handle garment measurement when garment has heavy stretch?
**A:** Measurement of stretch garments must be done WITHOUT tension — laid flat and relaxed. Inspector must not stretch the garment during measuring. If spec includes 'relaxed' and 'stretched' measurements, both must be taken separately using correct method.

### Q: What is 'lot integrity' and why does it matter for AQL?
**A:** Lot integrity = all pieces in the inspection lot must be from the same production run, same style, same size, and same PO. Mixing different batches, sizes, or styles in one AQL lot is invalid. Different production runs may have different quality levels. Mixed lots make defect attribution impossible.

### Q: What is the correct measurement for a polo shirt collar height deviation?
**A:** Polo shirt collar height: tolerance typically ±3mm from spec. Check both sides of collar. Inspector measures from collar attachment seam to collar edge. Both collar height and collar stand height may be specified. Record all measurements.

### Q: For Renner hangers, what information is typically printed/embossed on the hanger?
**A:** Renner hangers typically show: Renner brand name, hanger size code (numeric), and sometimes the product category indicator. The hanger specification document defines exact text, font, size, color, and placement of markings. Inspector must match physical hanger against the hanger specification.

### Q: What is the minimum elasticity requirement for waistband elastic in Renner childrenswear?
**A:** Waistband elastic must maintain minimum 2:1 stretch ratio (must stretch to twice its relaxed length). Elastic that cannot stretch adequately = MAJOR functional defect. Inspector tests by gentle manual stretch on 3 samples. Elastic that breaks or does not return to original length = CRITICAL/MAJOR.

### Q: What does 'pilling Grade 3' mean on the international scale?
**A:** Grade 3 (Moderate Change) = moderate pilling present on fabric surface. Pills are clearly visible and numerous but surface texture is still reasonably intact. Grade 3 is generally the minimum acceptable. Grades 1-2 = significant pilling = MAJOR defect. Grade 4-5 = excellent, slight or no pilling.

### Q: For Renner logistics, what is the acceptable weight variance on master carton GW label?
**A:** Acceptable variance: typically ±5% of declared Gross Weight. Variance exceeding ±5% = logistics defect requiring carton relabeling. Inspector must weigh sample cartons and compare to declared GW on master carton label.

### Q: What is a 'blind stitch hem' and what quality points are checked?
**A:** Blind stitch hem = a hem where the stitch is not visible from the garment exterior (used in trousers, skirts, coats). Quality checks: stitch visibility from outside (should be invisible or minimal), hem width consistency, hem fold evenness, and no thread catches or puckering at hem.

### Q: How does the inspection differ for a Pre-Production Sample (PPS) vs Final Inspection (FRI)?
**A:** PPS: evaluates single proto/pre-production sample for spec conformance BEFORE bulk production. No AQL. Objective: approve or reject design/construction. FRI: evaluates bulk production using AQL sampling. Objective: accept or reject the shipment. Different purposes, different methods.

### Q: What are 'float threads' in woven fabric and how are they classified?
**A:** Float threads = warp or weft threads that go over or under more threads than intended, creating a loose floating thread on the fabric surface. Classification: short floats (1-2mm) = MINOR. Long floats (>2mm, visible) = MAJOR. Very long floats (>5mm) = CRITICAL as they snag easily.

### Q: What is a 'needle cut' defect and how does it occur?
**A:** Needle cut = a small hole or tear in the fabric caused by the sewing needle. Occurs when: wrong needle gauge for fabric, blunt needle, or needle hitting a seam/obstruction. Classification: in visible area = MAJOR. In hidden area = MINOR. Multiple needle cuts = systematic problem, HOLD.

### Q: For Renner children's accessories (jewelry, hair accessories), what is the small parts rule?
**A:** Products for children under 3 years: NO small parts that can be detached and fit entirely in a small parts cylinder (31.7mm diameter × 57.1mm deep). This is per EN 71-1 and ASTM F963 toy/accessory safety standards. Any detachable small part = CRITICAL safety defect.

### Q: What is the 'Inspector's Decision Tree' for an ambiguous defect?
**A:** Decision tree: 1. Does it affect safety? → If yes = CRITICAL. 2. Does it affect function? → If yes = MAJOR. 3. Is it clearly visible to consumer from 50cm? → If yes = MAJOR. 4. Is it a slight imperfection, barely noticeable? → MINOR. 5. Still uncertain → consult QC office + defect code manual.

### Q: How does heat transfer print durability relate to inspection?
**A:** Heat transfer print durability is checked by peel test: attempt to peel edge of print. If print detaches easily = MAJOR durability defect. Also check for: wash fastness (per lab test), color vibrancy vs sealed sample, print position deviation (tolerance typically ±1cm), and print alignment on symmetric designs.

### Q: What is 'garment weight tolerance' and does inspector check it in FRI?
**A:** Garment weight tolerance = allowed variance in finished garment weight from spec. While fabric GSM is a lab test point, FRI inspectors generally do not weigh individual garments unless specifically required. Inspector focuses on visual, dimensional, and functional quality. Weight testing is typically lab-based.

### Q: What are the four types of zippers typically found in Renner garments?
**A:** 1. VISLON (plastic coil): lightweight, common in sportswear. 2. METAL: heavy, durable, used in denim and formal wear. 3. INVISIBLE/CONCEALED: used in dresses and skirts. 4. OPEN-END (separating): used in jackets and outerwear. Each has different quality checks (slider smoothness, bottom stop, chain quality, teeth alignment).

### Q: For a Renner accessories inspection (sunglasses), what UV protection verification is required?
**A:** Inspector verifies: UV400 protection label/sticker present and legible, lens tint level matches sealed sample, frame shape and measurements within tolerance, nose pad symmetry, hinge functionality (opens/closes smoothly), and lens optical quality (no distortion, bubbles, or scratches beyond tolerance).

### Q: What is 'hem turn up' and what are common defects?
**A:** Hem turn up = the fabric fold that forms the hem at garment bottom. Common defects: uneven hem width (varies around garment), hem folding direction wrong, visible raw edge showing through, hem puckering, blind stitch showing from exterior, and incorrect finished length due to wrong hem turn-up width.

### Q: How does the inspector verify 'pocket depth' compliance?
**A:** Inspector measures from pocket opening to the pocket bag bottom with a ruler inserted into the pocket. Minimum depth must allow hand/contents entry as per spec. Inspector measures 3-5 garments from AQL sample. Deviation from spec ±0.5-1cm may be allowed per tolerance guidelines.

### Q: What is a 'tack button' and what are inspection check points?
**A:** Tack button (rivet/tack) = metal button hammer-fixed on denim at stress points (pocket corners, fly, etc.). Check points: button must be flush with fabric (not raised or uneven), Renner brand mark visible on correct side, button must not detach under 90N pull test, no sharp edges, correct color/finish per spec.

### Q: What is 'side seam twist' tolerance in Renner menswear trousers?
**A:** Side seam twist tolerance: typically maximum 1-1.5cm visible twist when garment laid flat. Twist beyond tolerance = MAJOR construction defect (fabric alignment problem during cutting/sewing). Inspector checks by laying trousers flat and observing if side seam runs straight.

### Q: How is 'collar band height' checked for Renner men's formal shirts?
**A:** Collar band height measured: from neckline seam to collar stand top edge, measured at center front and center back. Tolerance typically ±2mm. Both sides must be equal. Unequal or out-of-tolerance collar band = MAJOR POM defect.

### Q: What is the role of 'lab test certificates' in Renner inspections?
**A:** Lab test certificates: documents from accredited laboratories confirming product compliance with chemical, physical, and safety standards. Inspector should verify: certificate is valid (not expired), covers the correct style/fabric lot, passes all required test parameters. Missing or expired lab certificate = HOLD pending valid certificate.

### Q: What is a 'snap fastener pull test' and what force is required?
**A:** Snap fastener pull test: applies tensile force to test if snap opens unintentionally. Requirement: snap must withstand minimum 90N pull force before opening for adult garments. For children's garments, same 90N minimum applies per safety standards. Testing done with pull gauge. Failure = MAJOR/CRITICAL defect.

### Q: In Renner inspection, what does 'AQL sampling by style or by colorway' mean?
**A:** If a PO has multiple colorways of the same style: some buyers require AQL sampling per colorway (each color gets its own sample). Others allow combined sampling if colorways share same construction. Renner may require colorway separation for color-critical or complex pattern styles. Check spec/manual for guidance.

### Q: What is the 'measurement relaxation time' before taking POM measurements on knit garments?
**A:** Knit garments should be allowed to relax for minimum 30 minutes (some specs require 24 hours) on a flat surface before measuring. Freshly folded or stretched knits may measure differently due to fabric memory tension. Measuring too soon = unreliable measurements.

### Q: For Renner logistics, what is the minimum information required on an individual polybag?
**A:** Individual polybag minimum info: article number, size, color (if single size per bag), barcode, and country of origin. Some bags also include: fiber composition, care icons, price (if pre-priced), Renner branding per spec. Missing mandatory information = LOGISTICS defect.

### Q: What is 'inline quality assurance rate' and what threshold triggers a stop?
**A:** IQA rate = percentage of defective pieces found during inline inspection. Typically: below 2-3% = PASS, continue production. 3-5% = WARNING, reinspect more frequently. Above 5-8% = STOP PRODUCTION for root cause analysis. Exact thresholds per Renner/QC office guidelines.

### Q: What does 'fabric grain line' mean and why does it matter in garment QC?
**A:** Grain line = the direction of warp threads (vertical, running parallel to selvage). Garment pieces must be cut on the correct grain line per pattern design. Off-grain cutting causes: garment twisting/torquing after wash, uneven drape, seam puckering, and visual distortion. Inspector checks grain alignment visually on finished garments.

### Q: What is the Renner policy on outsourced production?
**A:** When a factory subcontracts or outsources production to another factory without prior buyer approval = MAJOR compliance issue. Inspector should be alert to signs of production from different factories in the same lot (different fabric lots, different labels, different construction quality). Must be reported to QC office.

### Q: How does inspector verify 'button spacing' on a shirt placket?
**A:** Button spacing check: measure distance between each button/buttonhole from center to center. Compare all intervals to spec. Variation >±2mm between intervals = MAJOR visual defect (uneven button placement). Check both button and buttonhole rows for alignment.

### Q: What is 'open seam' defect and how is it found?
**A:** Open seam = a seam that has not been properly sewn, leaving an unsewn gap. Detection: gently pull apart the seam with fingertips or use a flat ruler to probe seam. Open seams in stress areas (armhole, crotch, shoulder) = CRITICAL. In non-stress areas = MAJOR. Any open seam is a quality failure.

### Q: What is the final 'Four Pillars' verification sequence recommended for an inspector?
**A:** Recommended sequence: 1. COMPARE WITH SEALED SAMPLE first (overall assessment). 2. AQL QUALITY CHECK (defect inspection on sample pieces). 3. POM MEASUREMENTS (dimensional check on 5-10 pieces). 4. RFID/BARCODE SCAN (verify all codes). 5. LOGISTICS CHECK (hangers, polybag, carton, labels, carton count). Report when all 4 pillars complete.
